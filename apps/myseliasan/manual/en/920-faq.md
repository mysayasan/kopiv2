# Frequently asked questions

## Does anything leave my network? {#egress}

No. The control plane answers from its own tables and its own built-in manuals, the language model
(if you enable one) runs on your network, and reports are rendered on the appliance itself.

Two things reach the internet, both only if you ask them to: **downloading a map region**, and
**downloading a model or the inference server**. Both have offline alternatives — a prepared
basemap file, and [Import](language-model#air-gap) from a file you carried in — and downloads can
be switched off entirely.

## Can I run this fully air-gapped? {#air-gap}

Yes, and it is the case the product is designed around. The manual is compiled into the binary,
retrieval needs no model, the basemap is a local file, and reports need no external renderer.

## What happens to my recorders if the control plane goes down? {#outage}

They keep working. A node keeps recording and detecting on its own — pairing adds central
management, it does not move the recording, the detection or the footage anywhere.

What you lose while the control plane is down is the central view: the combined feed, the map,
[fleet rules](fleet-rules) that correlate across nodes, and the digest.

## Where is the video actually stored? {#video}

On the node that recorded it. The control plane keeps the **event rows**, not the footage, and
plays clips by streaming them from the node over the control channel.

That is why a released or wiped node takes its clips with it while its event history stays here.

## Do I need a language model? {#need-model}

No. Alerts, [fleet rules](fleet-rules), the feed and the digest's findings are all computed without
one — the findings are ordinary code reading your tables, not model output.

A model adds the readable narrative and the [assistant](ask-the-fleet). Without one you can still
search the manuals and get the sections that answer a question.

## Can the assistant change anything? {#assistant-act}

No. It reads and answers. It cannot adopt a node, change a rule, acknowledge an alert or restart
anything.

## Why does my menu look different from a colleague's? {#menus}

Because the navigation is drawn from the same permission matrix that guards the API. You see a
section when your role has access to it, so a shorter menu means fewer grants — not a different
version of the software. See [Users, roles and access](users-and-roles#menus).

## Somebody signed in and saw nothing. Is that a bug? {#pending}

No, it is the intended behaviour. A new sign-in arrives with **no role** and waits for an
administrator to assign one. Authenticating proves identity; it does not grant access.

## Why does a camera only get one pin? {#one-pin}

Because it is in one physical place. Two pins would mean the system believes a device is in two
rooms, and every location-based answer built on that — the map, the reports, an alert naming a
room — would quietly be wrong. See [Buildings and floor plans](buildings-and-floors#pinning).

## Can two control planes manage the same node? {#two-parents}

No. An adopted node is locked to one control plane and stops answering discovery entirely, which is
what stops a second control plane quietly adopting an appliance that already belongs to somebody.

## How do I hand a node to a different control plane? {#rehome}

**Release** it here — the tidy route — then adopt it from the other one. Use **Wipe** only when you
are decommissioning the appliance or handing the hardware to somebody else; it erases the node's
recordings, cameras, rules, users and settings.

## Why did I get an intrusion alert for a normal entry? {#false-intrusion}

The rule is probably missing its **absence**, or its grace delay is too short for your badge
reader — readers routinely report a second or two behind the door contact they just authorised.
See [Fleet rules](fleet-rules#absence).
