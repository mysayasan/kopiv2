# A tour of the workspace

Once setup is finished, every screen shares the same frame: a navigation rail down one side and a
thin action strip across the top.

## The side rail {#side-rail}

The rail is the whole menu. It is grouped, and the groups are the product's own division of
labour.

**Workspace**

- **Dashboard** — the summary screen. Recent activity, which cameras are healthy, how much
  detection has been happening and whether that is normal for this time of day.
- **Live Views** — the video wall. Cameras tiled in a grid you arrange, paged when there are more
  cameras than tiles.

**Surveillance**

- **Cameras** — the only entry that expands. Its root opens camera discovery; each camera beneath
  it opens that camera's own pages. A coloured dot on each shows whether it is reachable right now,
  and a small badge appears while a camera is being taught a new skill. Past eight cameras the
  branch gains a filter box, so a long list stays usable.

**Intelligence**

- **Teach** — teach a camera to recognise something the stock model does not know.
- **People** — the face gallery.
- **Objects** — search what your cameras have seen, independently of whether any rule fired.

**System**

- **Notifications** — the alert and event feed. It carries an unread count, and it is the entry
  most people live in.
- **Settings** — everything configurable, in tabs.
- **Help** — this manual.

### Why your menu may be shorter {#menu-differences}

The rail shows what your role allows. A viewer sees Dashboard, Live Views, Notifications and Help
and nothing else; an operator sees the same plus recorded footage; an administrator sees all of it.

Nothing is merely hidden. The server refuses the request too, so a hidden area is genuinely
unavailable rather than one URL away. If you expected an entry and it is not there, it is your
role, not a fault — see [What MyMataSan is](welcome#roles).

### Pinning the rail {#pinning}

The pin at the top of the rail switches between **pinned** (always open) and **auto-hide** (slides
away, reappears when you move to the edge). Auto-hide buys back screen width, which is worth it on
the video wall and rarely worth it anywhere else. The choice is remembered.

## The account block {#account}

Under the logo sits your role and a sign-out button. It shows the *role*, not the username: an
appliance often sits on a screen other people can see, and there is no reason to advertise which
account is signed in.

## The top strip {#header}

Two controls, both instant and both remembered on this browser:

- **Language** — English, Malay, Chinese and Arabic. Arabic mirrors the whole layout.
- **Theme** — light or dark. Dark is the sensible choice for a control room at night; light
  survives a sunlit office better.

A **?** button next to them opens this manual at the page for whatever you are looking at, without
leaving the screen you are on.

## Things that appear over the top {#overlays}

- **Toasts** — brief confirmations in a corner. They disappear on their own.
- **The codec warning** — a banner saying live video is being converted, or dropped, because the
  browser cannot play the camera's stream directly. Clicking it takes you to the runtime settings
  that fix it. It is a performance warning, not an error.
- **Full-screen progress** — shown only for operations that must not be interrupted, such as a
  factory reset. Leave the page open; it reloads by itself when the appliance is back.
